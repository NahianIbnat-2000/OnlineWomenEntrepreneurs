# Online Women Entrepreneurs during the COVID-19 Pandemic

**A study on Chittagong City, Bangladesh**

Undergraduate research paper for ECON3253: Research Methodology, Asian University for Women (Fall 2021), by Nahian Ibnat.

## About the study

COVID-19 pushed Bangladesh's unemployment rate from 4.20% in 2019 to 5.30% in 2020 (World Bank). During the same period, many women in Chittagong started businesses online. This paper asks two questions:

1. How are online businesses influencing new women entrepreneurs in Chittagong during the pandemic?
2. Is online business effective enough to continue after COVID-19?

No earlier study had looked at Bangladeshi women running businesses online, so this paper is an initial step into that gap.

## Data and methods

- **Sample:** 50 women entrepreneurs in Chittagong City, selected by convenience sampling from the author's Facebook follow list. Response rate: 89.2%.
- **Collection:** a self-completion Google Form shared by email, Messenger and WhatsApp, with mostly closed questions and two open-ended ones.
- **Design:** quantitative, cross-sectional.
- **Tools:** STATA and Google Sheets.
- **Analysis:** frequency tables, charts, Pearson's r, Cramér's V, Spearman's rho, and an OLS regression with a White test for heteroscedasticity.

## Main findings

- 60% of respondents started their business during COVID-19, and cloth and jewellery was the most common business (50%).
- 66% had a housemaid and 64% received family support, which freed up time for business work.
- Median start-up investment and median monthly profit were both BDT 10,001 to 20,000.
- 78% want to continue after COVID-19, and 84% say continuing is possible.
- Business type, housemaid availability and family support did not significantly explain the decision to continue (R² = 14.5%, F = 1.16 against a critical value of 2.81).

## Limitations

The convenience sample, small sample size and cross-sectional design mean the findings cannot be generalised to all women entrepreneurs in Chittagong, and no causal claims can be made. The paper recommends a larger random sample, logistic or probit models, and causal designs in future work.
