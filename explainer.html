A study on Chittagong City, Bangladesh

Why it matters

COVID-19 pushed unemployment up, yet online business kept growing

4.20% → 5.30%

Bangladesh unemployment, 2019 to 2020 (World Bank).

Not everyone can work online, but people who want to run a business and know the technology can. Studies from other countries found that online business creates opportunities for women, who can manage a business and household duties together.

The questions

Two questions, one gap in the research

Question 1

How are online businesses influencing new women entrepreneurs in Chittagong during the pandemic?

Question 2

Is online business effective enough to continue after COVID-19?

The gap: studies covered women business owners in Canada, the UK, Pakistan and Bangladesh, but none covered Bangladeshi women who operate online.

What drives the decision

The factors tested don't explain who continues

Cramér's V with continuing

Housemaid.36
Business type.26

All weak or very weak.

Regression

R² = 14.5%. F = 1.16, below the critical 2.81, so the model is not significant. The White test finds no heteroscedasticity (p = 0.66).

Profit and weekly business hours: r = 0.114, very weak.

These factors may help women start online businesses, but in this sample they do not predict who continues.

Honest limits

What the study can and can't say

Limits

  • Convenience sample, so it does not generalise to all women entrepreneurs in Chittagong.
  • Small sample, constrained by time and cost.
  • No causal claims: no treatment and control groups.

Next steps

  • A larger random sample with a proper sampling frame.
  • Logistic or probit regression for the yes/no decision.
  • Causal designs such as randomised trials.

Takeaway

A real start, and a question still open

Many women in Chittagong started online businesses during COVID-19, helped by housemaids and family support. Most are satisfied and want to continue. The factors tested explain little of that decision, so what drives it remains to be found.
